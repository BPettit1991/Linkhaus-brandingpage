01 · Foundation

What Linkhaus is, and who it is for.

Linkhaus is an AI and operations partner for Australian businesses. We work out what is worth automating, build it into the tools you already use, and keep it running. One accountable operator, from the decision to the thing that works.

The thesis

Most businesses don't have an AI problem. They have a shipping problem. The ambition is there and the tools exist. What's missing is the line from a good decision to a running system. Linkhaus is that line.

Who we are for

Launch focus

Trades and hospitality owners

Owner-operators with 2 to 15 staff, and small venues: plumbers, electricians, builders, cafes, restaurants and takeaways. They lose hours each week to quoting, invoice chase-ups, rostering, bookings and posting. They want it handled, not a new app to learn.

Also

Teams past the pilot stage

Founders and operators who have decided to use AI and need it built and running in production. The buyer is the person accountable for the outcome: an owner, a head of ops, a manager with a budget.

Not for: teams shopping for a proof of concept to justify a budget, or anyone who wants a big firm's logo on the cover more than they want the thing built.

Lead with whichever pillar fits the audience. Never blend all three into one vague sentence.

Strategy that ships

Advice you can build on. Every recommendation comes with the path to production attached, costed and sequenced.

Pillar 2

Systems, not slideware

We hand over working systems inside the tools you already use, not a PDF to interpret. The deliverable is something that's on.

Pillar 3

Built to last

Documented, owned by you, and still running after we step back. No black boxes, no lock-in, no new logins you didn't ask for.

02 · Voice and tone

A competent operator explaining a system to a peer.

Linkhaus writes the way it builds: plainly, and only what's load-bearing. Confident, specific, never hyped.

Personality

Direct, not blunt

Lead with the point, then the proof. Say what you'll do and what it costs. Be kind about it.

Specific, not technical

Name the task, the tool and the number. Skip jargon unless the reader uses it first.

Calm, not hyped

AI is a tool, not a miracle. We describe what it does, where it's good, and where plain automation is the better choice.

Accountable, not salesy

One person who answers. Say "I" or "we" and own the outcome. Admit limits plainly: "We don't offer that yet."

Rules

  1. Point first. The first sentence carries the message. No throat-clearing, no "In today's fast-paced world".
  2. Short sentences, active voice, plain words. "We set up your quote follow-ups", not "Quote follow-up processes will be implemented".
  3. Checkable or cut. Every claim is a number, a named task or a thing the reader can see working.
  4. Their words, not ours. Write "chasing unpaid invoices", not "accounts receivable workflow optimisation".
  5. Honest about limits. Say what we don't do and why. It builds more trust than any adjective.

Say this, not that

Not this

We leverage cutting-edge AI to unlock seamless, transformative operational efficiencies.

This

We set up your quote follow-ups so they go out on time, every time. Most owners get back 4 to 6 hours a week.

Website, services section. Use a real figure only once you have one from a client.

Not this

Hi! Hope you're well! I'd love to jump on a quick call to explore synergies between our businesses!

This

Hi Sam, I noticed your quotes go out from a shared inbox. I automate follow-ups for trades businesses like yours. Worth a 15-minute call this week?

Cold outreach email, first lines.

Not this

🚀 Game-changing AI is here to revolutionise hospitality!!! 🔥

This

A cafe we work with used to spend Sunday night on the roster. Now it builds itself from availability, and the owner checks it in five minutes.

Social post. Describe a real (permitted) example; never invent one.

04 · Logo

The name, drawn as one line.

The icon is a single stroke that never lifts. It starts at the foot of an L, runs up the stem and keeps going: the top of the letter becomes the peak of a roof, closing on a short return that reads as a chimney. Set into the chimney is one small window, lit in Haus Green.

The rule: the window is the mark's only accent. Everything else is ink on light backgrounds, white on dark ones. The window never moves and never appears twice.

Rules

  • Headlines in Baloo 2, sentence case. Never set Baloo 2 in all caps or below 18 px.
  • Body text 16 px minimum on screen, 9.5 pt minimum in print. Keep lines to about 65 characters.
  • Uppercase only for short mono labels, with 0.06 to 0.08 em letter-spacing.
  • Use tabular numbers (Inter or JetBrains Mono) wherever figures line up in columns.
  • No other typefaces. If a platform can't load the fonts (some email clients), fall back to Arial for headings and body.

09 · Imagery

Real work, real places, real screens.

Use
  • Clients' real workplaces: a site ute, a cafe pass, an office desk (with written permission).
  • Screenshots of the systems we build, with customer data blurred or replaced.
  • Natural light, honest colour, people mid-task rather than posed.
  • Typographic posts (ink, white, one green word) when there's no good photo.
Avoid
  • Robots, glowing brains, blue circuit boards, "digital" particle swirls.
  • Generic stock handshakes and laptop-on-a-beach shots.
  • AI-generated people presented as staff or clients.
  • Green colour washes over photos. Green belongs to type and the mark.
Licensing. Use only photos we took, photos a client has given written permission for, or properly licensed stock. Record where every image came from.
10 · Motion

Quiet and functional, like the voice.

Fast · 120 ms

Hover, focus, small state changes.

Base · 200 ms

Toggles, toasts, reveals.

Slow · 400 ms

Page-level transitions, the logo sting.

Easing: cubic-bezier(0.22, 0.61, 0.36, 1). Things move to show cause and effect, never to perform. In video, the mark can draw itself along its single line, then the window lights. Always respect reduced-motion settings.

12 · Pre-publish checklist

Before anything goes out.

  • The first sentence or headline carries the point.
  • Every claim is a number, a named task, or something the reader can see. Nothing invented.
  • None of the "avoid" words. No em dashes. Australian spelling.
  • The name is "Linkhaus". Phone 0480 043 497, email hello@linkhaus.com.au and ABN 39 614 990 330 are exactly right.
  • No certification, licensing or "coming soon" claims we can't back.
  • The right logo version for the background, with clear space and above minimum size.
  • Haus Green used as a signal only; green text on dark uses Haus Green 400.
  • Text passes contrast; body text at least 16 px on screen.
  • Images are ours, permitted or licensed, and recorded.
  • One clear call to action that says exactly what happens.
